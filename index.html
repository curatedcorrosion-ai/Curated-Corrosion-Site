Curated Corrosion studies, documents, and preserves electronic component specimens before carefully returning selected examples to useful life through experimental audio circuits and handcrafted instruments.

“Every component deserves the respect of being understood.”

The archive begins with curiosity: identify the specimen, preserve its provenance, characterize its behavior, document what can be supported by evidence, and observe what happens when it returns to a circuit.

Curator's Handbook

The language and standards of the Workshop.

A living reference describing how Curated Corrosion identifies, documents, studies, uses, and — when necessary — deaccessions component specimens.

Specimen

An individual electronic component admitted to the Curated Corrosion collection for preservation, study, characterization, experimentation, or eventual use.

A specimen is more than a part number. Manufacturer, markings, physical condition, provenance, measured characteristics, and observed behavior may distinguish one specimen from another nominally identical device.

Accession

The formal act of admitting a component specimen into the collection and assigning it a persistent Curated Corrosion identity.

The physical accession card remains deliberately simple: Specimen ID, Type/Family, Date/Era, Manufacturer/Markings, Provenance, Condition/Notes, and Storage Location.

Provenance

Source — Date Acquired — Location

Provenance records how a specimen entered the collection. More detailed acquisition records may include seller, purchase price, lot photography, packaging, correspondence, and associated material.

Curator's Observation

A subjective observation derived from careful bench or sonic evaluation. Observation is intentionally separated from independently verifiable historical or electrical fact.

Measurements are data. Listening impressions are observations. Both deserve documentation, but they are not the same kind of evidence.

Stewardship

Component specimens are not acquired as commodities for individual resale. Once documented, specimens remain within the Curated Corrosion collection or are incorporated into complete Curated Corrosion instruments.

Deaccession

The deliberate removal of a specimen from the active collection when it does not serve the collection's mission, lacks required functionality, substantially duplicates existing holdings, or otherwise has a documented reason for release.

Transparency

The archive distinguishes what is known, what is measured, what is observed, and what remains uncertain. Unknown manufacturer marks, approximate eras, and unresolved historical questions are recorded honestly rather than converted into unsupported certainty.

About Curated Corrosion

Equal parts laboratory, workshop, and museum.

Curated Corrosion began with a simple interest in building guitar pedals and quickly became something broader: an effort to understand the history and behavior of the components that made those circuits possible.

The project is built around deliberate curiosity. Vintage transistors, capacitors, diodes, integrated circuits, and other electronic artifacts are identified, preserved, characterized, researched, and documented before selected specimens return to useful life inside handmade audio instruments.

The Gallery exists as a public learning resource. The Workshop turns that research into experiments. The finished instruments are artifacts of that process rather than its sole purpose.

“The more we understand the specimen, the more meaningful its next chapter becomes.”
